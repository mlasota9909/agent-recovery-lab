## Objective

Prove from the **decoded stream** that the three properties the experiment depends on actually hold: before-images are
present, DDL appears in correct LSN order relative to DML, and heartbeats give a usable time index.

T004 configured these. T014 proves them end to end. Configuration passing is not evidence that the stream carries what
you need.

## Steps

1. Start a host-side `pg_recvlogical` capture into a scratch `db.jsonl`.
2. **Before-image proof (AC-6).** Insert a row with known content, commit, then `DELETE` it. In the decoded stream,
   locate the delete record and confirm it contains the **full old tuple**, not just the key. Show the actual JSON.
   This is what makes "damaged data retained" scorable; without it that metric is unmeasurable.
3. **UPDATE before/after proof.** Update a row and confirm both old and new values appear. SC-04's whole detection
   story depends on this.
4. **DDL ordering proof (AC-7).** Execute an interleaved sequence: `INSERT`, `CREATE TABLE`, `INSERT`, `DROP TABLE`,
   `INSERT`. Then confirm the corresponding `lab_ddl_audit` records appear in the stream **in the correct position
   relative to the surrounding DML**, by LSN. Ordering is the point: a DDL trail that arrives out of order cannot
   support SC-03.
5. **Heartbeat density proof (AC-14).** Over a 120 s window, confirm heartbeat records appear at ~1 Hz in the stream
   with non-decreasing LSN, and demonstrate a wall-clock-to-LSN lookup using only stream contents.
6. **Transaction boundary check.** Confirm `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` boundaries and commit LSNs are recoverable, and that a
   rolled-back transaction does **not** appear as committed change. An analysis engine that counted aborted work as
   damage would produce false coordinates.
7. Record the stream's volume characteristics: bytes per second and records per second under steady-state load. P5's
   representation work needs this to size summaries, and it tells you whether the flat-log arm is even feasible to feed
   to a small model.

## Negative test (required)

Set `REPLICA IDENTITY DEFAULT` on `lab_ledger`, delete a row, and show the decoded record now carries **only the key** —
demonstrating concretely that the `FULL` setting is load-bearing and that the before-image assertion genuinely detects
its absence. Restore `FULL`, re-verify, and capture both stream excerpts side by side. This is the single most
convincing artefact in the tranche.

## Review bundle

Standard five, plus `before-image-proof.json` (the actual decoded records), `ddl-ordering-proof.json`,
`heartbeat-window.json`, `rollback-absent-proof.json`, `stream-volume.md`,
`negative-replica-identity-default.json`.

## Operator review checklist

- [ ] Real decoded JSON shown, not a claim that it works
- [ ] Before-images present for both DELETE and UPDATE
- [ ] DDL ordering correct relative to DML
- [ ] Aborted transactions excluded
- [ ] Negative test shows key-only records without `REPLICA IDENTITY FULL`
- [ ] Stream volume recorded for P5 sizing
